This morning's discussion about extreme flat management trends in Silicon Valley sparked reflections on organizational structure and human nature. In state-owned enterprises and similar institutions, middle layers serve not only managerial functions but also as responsibility distribution mechanisms—a practical necessity that aligns with human nature. While AI could theoretically enable hyper-flat management, fundamental institutional adjustments are prerequisites. More importantly, at the individual level: in the AI era, what truly matters are learning ability, curiosity, and emotional value—things that AI cannot replace. The human element is like the "eye" in the "finishing touch" idiom—without it, everything loses its essence.

## Fragmented Thoughts

- Some Silicon Valley companies now have one person directly managing 6000+ employees, completely eliminating middle layers—this is becoming a trend
- Middle layers in state-owned enterprises aren't just for management; they're responsibility distribution mechanisms—aligning with human nature, providing hierarchy to share blame rather than piling it all on top
- AI-enabled extreme flatness is logically sound, but implementation requires fundamental institutional restructuring (top-down push or systematic overhaul)
- The key question for individuals: how to learn things AI cannot replace in the AI era, letting AI support personal goals
- Curiosity and inquisitiveness are things AI will never replace
- Emotional value is also hard for AI to substitute
- The "finishing touch" metaphor: the human element is that "eye"—without it, even the best content lacks essence, like a dish without salt
- Learning never stops
