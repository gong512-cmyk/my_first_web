GitHub Copilot got banned last night. After digging around, I suspect it was the proxy switch that triggered the risk control system — I had been using a Tencent proxy, then switched to a North American one, and the IP change was drastic enough to lock me out. I still have no idea how to get it back. Top up? Buy a membership? Nothing is clear.

It stings a bit. Using foreign models from within China is riddled with obstacles — even switching proxies can get your account flagged. The silver lining is that domestic models are catching up. Zhipu has aggressive rate limiting and Alibaba's Qwen is hard to get a slot on, but at least there are options. I really hope DeepSeek V4 running on domestic hardware matures fast, so ordinary people can access quality models affordably.

This afternoon I read a blog by a university professor, built on Naval Ravikant's ideas, about how ordinary people can adapt quickly in the AI era. Four core points, each one landing hard.

First: AI hits the middle layer hardest — not the top or the bottom. Jobs in small and medium-sized companies are the most replaceable. Ordinary people should dive deep into a niche, let AI amplify their expertise, and refine their craft to an edge.

Second: the pathways to knowledge will open up like never before. AI can pinpoint the boundary of your understanding and flood you with exactly what you need to learn next. The key is knowing how to ask questions — new angles, new questions — and then cross-validating what you learn. His analogy: don't learn to fix the carriage. Build a new car.

Third: how to deal with the anxiety of an AI explosion. Naval's answer is "aggressive action." Anxiety is fear of the unknown, and the cure is motion — not clarity. Don't wait until the plan is perfect. Just start. This is the same spirit as "learn by doing, do by learning."

Fourth: human advantage is still real. Desire and curiosity. No matter how powerful AI gets, how vast its knowledge, how many functions it gains — in the near term, these remain ours. Keeping desire and curiosity alive will matter for a long time.

Looking back, my own day mirrored all four: Copilot got banned, so I started testing domestic models. I read a blog and immediately distilled it into action items. That's aggressive action in its truest form.
