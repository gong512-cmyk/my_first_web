A mixed day—half tech, half management.

First, I looked into GitHub Copilot's subscription pricing. For users in China right now, it's arguably the most accessible platform—Claude and GPT integrated under one roof, no need for separate subscriptions. Good value. Worth keeping an eye on.

Then I used Hermes Agent to set up a personal database system. It handles document organization impressively well—classification, search, cross-referencing, all much faster than manual sorting. But finishing the setup actually left me more clear-headed than excited: the framework was never the point. The best tool in the world is just a pretty empty shell if you don't know how to fill it, how to use that knowledge to support thinking and decision-making. Building a second brain—the framework is only step one. Everything after that—how to capture, how to organize, how to review, how to create connections between ideas—those are the details that take sustained effort. This is long-term work. No shortcuts.

Over at the factory, two things worth noting. First, when the technicians hit a snag, their default instinct is to look outward—equipment problems, process problems, other people not cooperating. Rarely do they start by asking what they themselves haven't done well enough. This habit needs patient, persistent guidance—it won't change with a single talking-to, but the direction has to be held. Second, during the morning meeting, the boss emphasized the importance of automation in reducing human error. Zhuang Yuwei has been assigned to push this forward. Writing this down is the right call—important things shouldn't live only in memory.

## Key actions

- Researched GitHub Copilot subscription plans—confirmed it as the best multi-model platform for users in China
- Set up a personal database system with Hermes Agent; clarified that the framework is just a means—consistent curation is the real core
- Factory morning meeting: boss stressed automation to reduce human error; assigned Zhuang Yuwei to drive this
- Noted technicians' tendency to look outward when facing problems—long-term guidance toward internal attribution needed

## Fragments

"Second brain" has been a buzzword for years, but most of the conversation revolves around which tool to use—Obsidian vs Notion, Logseq vs Roam. The tool debate is the least important part. What's actually hard is building the habit loop: capture, organize, review, connect. Without that loop, even the best tool is just a digital graveyard—a pile of notes that will never be opened again.
