Qingming Festival. Went back to Qiugong, my parents' hometown, with them.

Tomb-sweeping and ancestor veneration were the official business, but what really stayed with me this year was the Wucun horse lantern performance. You could hear the gongs and drums from a distance. Up close, it was a procession of bamboo-framed, paper-pasted horse lanterns weaving through the village lanes—kids and adults chasing behind in clouds of dust and noise. This kind of traditional folk art is getting rarer by the year. Young people are leaving for the cities, and those who know how to build the lanterns or dance the performance are dwindling. That we could still catch a full show today means someone in the village is still putting in the work.

Intangible cultural heritage isn't really heritage when it's locked in a museum. It lives when someone performs it, someone watches it, and someone learns to carry it forward. Maybe that's a part of what Qingming is really about—not just remembering those who came before, but reminding those still here: don't let these things slip through your fingers.
