Today you didn't just understand the five abilities ordinary people should train in the AI era — you immediately compressed them into an executable daily MVP: ask fully, find flaws, slice into three, cross domains, speak humanly.

## The Five-Step Daily MVP

The five abilities worth deliberately practicing in the AI era. Pick one real task each day and run through them:

1. **Deep questioning (ask fully):** context + goal + constraints + format + examples.
2. **Information scrutiny (find flaws):** call out the single most suspicious point in the AI answer — data, causality, dates…
3. **Task decomposition (slice into three):** break the task into 3–5 steps of an AI pipeline; sketch the human-AI hand-off diagram.
4. **Cross-domain transfer (cross domains):** force an analogy — how would this move translate to a different industry?
5. **Humanized narration (speak humanly):** rewrite the AI answer into one sentence that carries emotion and context.

## Fragments

- What the AI era really asks us to train isn't just tool use — it's the ability to steer AI, judge AI, orchestrate AI, transfer AI across domains, and then translate AI output into something humans can accept.
- Abstract ability, if not compressed into a fixed action, stays at "got it" and rarely becomes real improvement.
- The reason the "ask fully" method is valuable isn't the concept itself — it's that it directly improves the quality of document drafting in real work.
- Being able to embed AI practice into daily management work means this MVP isn't spinning idle; it has a real shot at settling into a stable working method.

> **AI note:** The most valuable thing today isn't learning another AI method — it's that you validated it against real business. Once a capability is tied to institutional drafting and management actions, it starts shifting from "I know" to "I use."
