Today was a meaningful one — I sat down with my son and explored Claude's capabilities together, officially ushering him into the world of AI.

We started with Ohm's Law. He has been studying electricity recently, and the formulas in his textbook always felt abstract to him. I had Claude generate a dynamic animation on the spot, visually demonstrating the relationship between current, resistance, and voltage. Watching the current flow change speed as resistance shifted on screen, he got it immediately — far more effective than staring at formulas in a book.

Then came the highlight of the day: generating a game with a single sentence. I asked Claude to create a spaceship shooter game in one prompt, and it delivered — fully playable, complete visuals, smooth controls. My son watched the entire process, from natural language description to a running game, and his eyes lit up. That instant feedback loop from idea to reality is the most powerful spark for curiosity.

As a father, the best thing I can do is put the best tools in his hands. AI is not a monster to fear — it is the infrastructure of his generation. I hope he grows up to embrace this era deeply — not just using the tools, but understanding the thinking behind them; building his own knowledge base to systematically capture what he learns; and enriching his experience with the best AI tools available. Technology changes, but curiosity and the ability to keep learning will always be the core competitive edge.

## Key actions

- Used Claude to generate an Ohm's Law animation, helping my son grasp electrical concepts intuitively
- Created a playable shooter game from a single sentence, demonstrating the full journey from natural language to a complete program
- Had a deep conversation with my son about mindsets and learning attitudes in the AI era

## Fragments

- Visualization is the shortest path to understanding abstract concepts, and AI makes it effortless
- A child's excitement about instant creation is the best learning driver there is
- Putting the tools in your child's hands is worth more than a hundred lectures
