Today, for the first time, I used GitHub Copilot's autonomous coding capability to build a workout-logging app. In the Vibe Coding era — learn by doing, do while learning — just get started first.

---

## Fragments

- The Vibe Coding era favors learning-by-doing over thinking-first.
- Many problems aren't answered by thinking them through first; the answer appears in the process of doing.
- "Just start" is becoming a more effective mode of action.
- Things that used to require a programmer can now be done by non-specialists with AI help.
- AI will significantly disrupt highly replaceable jobs, but human curiosity and desire aren't easily replaced.
- The question worth dwelling on isn't just what AI can do, but what remains uniquely human.
- Understanding of AI tools has to be validated through real use — paper understanding isn't real mastery.
- When maintaining Obsidian, VS Code isn't just a writing tool but a maintenance hub that helps surface dead links, data mismatches, and structural issues in the knowledge base.
- When Claude Code's onboarding friction and environment constraints become a real blocker, moving to a smoother, more stable toolchain isn't a compromise — it's pragmatic iteration.

> **AI note:** Today wasn't just about learning a new tool — it was the start of forming your own judgment criteria for AI workflows: what's worth investing in, what's worth sticking with long-term, and what must be validated through real use. That shift is both grounded and clear-eyed.
