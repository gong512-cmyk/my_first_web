Today spanned quite a range—from factory safety to AI model selection—but the underlying logic is the same: it is all about judging return on investment.

During the day, I inspected routine safety management and found the safety officer's performance lacking. Two issues stood out. First, when pushing workshop teams to report hazards, they were padding numbers rather than genuinely discovering risks. Second, violation enforcement was low in both quantity and quality. These reflect two layers of failure: the safety officer's own diligence has slipped, and management has dropped the ball on safety oversight.

As a plant-level manager, I cannot just pay lip service to "safety first." But the reality is that professional staff—like safety officers—are often reluctant to crack down on workshop teams. It is performative work. Looks busy from the outside, but nobody actually holds anyone accountable when mistakes happen.

I arrived at a simple hard metric: to judge whether a manager is truly pushing work forward, look at how many penalty notices or reprimands they have issued to subordinate units over a given period. If an entire quarter goes by without a single penalty, a single assessment, a single warning—then their contribution to actually moving the needle is close to zero. This standard applies to myself as well. A reminder worth sharing.

In the evening, I switched channels. I saw this morning that Tongyi Qianwen's Coding Plan now supports the new Qwen 3.6 Plus model. I tested it for a while and it is seriously capable. Currently I am mainly on Zhipu GLM-5 Turbo, which also performs well on agent tasks—close to Sonnet 4.6 levels. But Zhipu's pricing is absurd, nearly 500 yuan a month. The value just is not there. Qianwen is currently in limited sale; I hope to get a slot soon and migrate fully from Zhipu to Qianwen.

This needs holistic planning. Beyond my own accounts (including the Hermes account), my wife uses them too, and the kids' accounts will need nurturing down the line. In the agent era, investing in AI is necessary—but it should be planned as a whole, not spent in scattered fragments. Here is hoping domestic LLMs keep improving—not just in capability, but in pricing too.

## Key actions
- Inspected routine safety management and reviewed safety officer performance
- Found clear deficiencies in both hazard identification and violation enforcement
- Tested Qwen 3.6 Plus and evaluated a model migration plan
- Began holistic planning of family AI subscription costs (self, wife, kids)
